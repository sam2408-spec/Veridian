Project 1

Expressions — where art speaks for your soul

A free art session for parents and children at the Light House Galley, Colombo, helping parents get to know their children through art.

About the project

Getting to know your child, one drawing at a time

Children often show in colour and shape what they cannot yet put into words. Expressions brought parents and children together around the same table, with paper, pastels and time — and invited parents to look closely at what their children were telling them.

Held free of charge at the Light House Galley in Colombo, the session combined a hands-on art activity with a practical talk on child psychology and parenting, led by Veridian Academy’s founder, Sameera Mahboobdeen.

Why it matters

Seeing, hearing and understanding our children

Recognising the child in front of you

Every child has their own temperament, pace and way of seeing the world. When parents take time to notice who their child actually is — their interests, worries and strengths — rather than who they expect them to be, children feel seen. Feeling seen is the foundation of self-worth and of the trust that lets a child come to a parent when something is wrong.

Building better communication

Good communication is less about talking more and more about listening well. Children open up when they are listened to without being rushed, judged or corrected straight away. Naming feelings, asking open questions, and staying calm when a child brings bad news teach children that difficult conversations are safe — a lesson that matters even more in the teenage years.

The psychology of good parenting

Decades of research point in the same direction. Children with a secure attachment to a caregiver handle stress better and form healthier relationships. Parenting that combines warmth with clear, consistent boundaries — often called authoritative parenting — is linked to better emotional, social and academic outcomes than parenting that is either harsh or permissive. Helping children understand and manage their emotions, rather than dismissing them, builds resilience.

What we discussed

Practical child psychology for everyday family life

Issues coming from school

How to recognise when a child is struggling with schoolwork, friendships, bullying or pressure — and how to respond in ways that support the child rather than add to the stress.

Maladaptive behaviour

Understanding behaviours such as tantrums, withdrawal, aggression or avoidance as signals of an unmet need, and responding to the cause rather than only the behaviour.

Communication methods

Active listening, naming emotions, age-appropriate explanations and calm, consistent boundaries that children can understand and trust.

Child psychology in practice

Simple tools parents can use at home — including art and play — to notice changes in a child’s mood and to open conversations that are hard to start with words alone.

Through art

Why art?

Art gives children a language before they have the words. A drawing can show fear, joy, confusion or pride — and gives a parent a gentle way into a conversation: “Tell me about this picture.” At Expressions, children drew while parents learned what to look for, so the activity and the lesson worked together.

Project 2 · Disaster relief

Cyclone Ditwah relief — standing with affected families

From the first days after the cyclone until the end of January 2026, Veridian supported people affected by Cyclone Ditwah with medical aid, relief materials and clothing.

This project was carried out in collaboration with the Free Palestine Movement Sri Lanka.

The disaster

One of Sri Lanka’s worst floods in decades

Cyclone Ditwah made landfall on 28 November 2025 and triggered the worst flooding Sri Lanka had seen in more than 20 years. United Nations reporting in early December recorded more than 600 deaths, over 200 people missing and more than two million people affected, with Gampaha, Colombo and Puttalam among the hardest-hit districts. Hundreds of thousands of people were forced into government-run safety centres.

Families arrived in those centres with little more than the clothes they were wearing — many needing medical attention, and all needing basic supplies.

Figures: UN Resident Coordinator, Sri Lanka; UN OCHA / ReliefWeb (December 2025).

What we did

Three strands of support, delivered together with the Free Palestine Movement Sri Lanka

Medical assistance in relief camps

Drawing on our founder’s training as a Medical First Responder, the team provided medical assistance in the relief camps in Wellampitiya — first aid, wound care and basic health checks for displaced families.

Medical aid and materials

Medical supplies and essential relief materials were gathered and delivered to people affected by the floods, helping families meet urgent needs in the weeks after the cyclone.

Clothes drives with Free Palestine Movement Sri Lanka

Together with Free Palestine Movement Sri Lanka, we ran two free clothes distributions for cyclone victims — collecting, sorting and labelling quality second-hand clothing for babies, children, teens, women and men.

Clothes distributions

Two drives at the Public Library, Colombo

  • 18 December 2025 · 3 pm – 6 pmPublic Library, Colombo
  • 31 December 2025 · 3 pm – 5 pmPublic Library, Colombo

Donated clothing was sorted by size and type — men’s trousers, women’s blouses and tops, boys’ clothing by age — so that families could quickly find what they needed. Both distributions were free of charge.

Our Ditwah support concluded at the end of January 2026.

Project 3 · Community health

Eduragala Estate medical camp

On Sunday, 2 August 2026, Veridian had the privilege of supporting the people of Eduragala Estate, Ingiriya, through a free medical camp.

Three clinics under one roof

Project 4 · Community health

Elpitiya Estate medical & eye camp

On 12 September 2026, Veridian held another medical and eye check-up camp, this time at Elpitiya Estate, Elpitiya, for the workers and residents who call it home.

From camp to care

Three stages, one commitment

12 September 2026 · The camp

Medical consultations and eye check-ups for estate workers and residents. Two doctors travelled to the camp grounds to see participants, and sponsors provided the medicines and glasses.

29 September 2026 · Final eye screening

The final stage of the eye screening was completed. Patients with retinal damage or cataract were referred to the nearest hospital eye clinic for further treatment.

19 November 2026 · Commitment fulfilled

Another community commitment towards the welfare of our people was brought to a close.

Why it matters

These are our people too

Estate communities often live far from proper health facilities. A single camp can find problems early, but people also need a path to treatment — which is why every patient with retinal damage or cataract was referred on to a hospital eye clinic.

Sri Lanka should provide annual medical camps like this one, in partnership with the corporate sector. Companies can take up these camps as their CSR projects, and help our people reach the health care they need.

“Let’s support the underprivileged.”Veridian Academy

Project 5 · Fundraiser for Gaza

An Afternoon of Art and Breath

On Sunday, 22 November 2026, a room in Colombo 5 filled with people who gave up their afternoon for an event with a purpose beyond the room.

The afternoon

Arriving, breathing, creating

The Sunflower Circle organised the fundraiser at The Fearless Space, and it began at 3 in the afternoon with a quiet start. Sameera Mahboobdeen, clinical psychologist and founder of Veridian Academy, led everyone through a relaxation session, guiding the room through deep breaths until the usual noise of the week began to settle. It was a gentle way to arrive, and it set the tone for the hours that followed.

Sameera then spoke about art and what it means to express ourselves through it — how a person’s mental state tends to show up in what they create, and how a piece can carry things a person has not yet found words for. She returned to the breathing exercises and showed how certain techniques can help the brain become more alert, giving everyone something practical to take home.

The conversation

Talking through the art

What made the afternoon feel so personal was the conversation. Participants sat with Sameera and talked through their own work, asking what it might be saying and what it revealed about them. That kind of exchange with a professional is rare, and it was a highlight of the day.

The event wrapped up a little after 5, but its purpose carries on.

Where the funds go

Al Noor Educational Classes, Gaza

All funds raised go to Al Noor Educational Classes, a school in Gaza, to help the school and its students through the winter. A school that stays open gives children a place to learn and a bit of routine — and everyone who came and took part helped make that possible.

“Everyone in that room came for an afternoon of art and left having done something for children they will probably never meet.”The Sunflower Circle

Founder & CEO

Sameera Mahboobdeen

Meet our founder

Where psychology meets practice

Sameera is a consultant in psychology, forensics and criminology, and one of the female Master Trainers in Asia for Medical First Responder – Disaster Response for the Asian Disaster Preparedness Centre (ADPC).

Across corporate, higher-education and emergency-response settings, every programme starts with the same question: what does this specific group of people need in order to think more clearly, work more safely, and grow with confidence, while loving what they do?

Evidence-based designInclusive facilitationMeasurable outcomesApplied research
“A training programme should be judged the same way a clinical intervention is — by whether it actually changes behaviour, not by whether it was well received.”Sameera Mahboobdeen

A background spanning clinical and developmental psychology, criminology and the criminal justice system, together with forensic medicine, shapes how each curriculum is built — grounded in evidence, tested against real learning outcomes, and designed to include people who have not always felt welcome in a classroom or a boardroom. Alongside the training work runs a continuing commitment to normalising conversations about mental health inside institutions that have historically avoided them.

How a programme is built

Who I work with

Sameera currently serves as Director – Training & Curriculum Development and Academic Management at Veridian Academy for Skill Enhancement in Colombo, a role she has held since 2023 alongside her practice as a Clinical and Forensic Psychologist.

Since 2022 she has been Course Monitor for CADRE Training at the Asian Disaster Preparedness Center, where she is also a Master Instructor in Disaster & Emergency Response. Since 2020 she has lectured in forensic, business and developmental psychology and criminology, and has coordinated the Diploma in Correction & Rehabilitation for Prison Officers at the University of Kelaniya.

From February 2022 to August 2023 she was Head of Academics & Educational Consultant at WinSYS Networks (Pvt) Ltd. Since 2017 she has served St John Ambulance Sri Lanka as a National Trainer and as Assistant District Commissioner for the Kalutara District.

Her independent research, ongoing since 2015, focuses on gender equality and equity, psychology, criminology and the criminal justice system. Her work in education began in 2008, teaching primary and secondary subjects.
